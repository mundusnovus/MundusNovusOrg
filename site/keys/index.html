Asteria Keys

Your passwords, only yours. And, yes, that is the entire pitch.

Keys keeps logins, passkeys, one-time codes, cards, identities, Wi-Fi passwords and secure notes in one encrypted vault. The vault is sealed on your device. Your master password never leaves it. Apple never gets the plaintext, and neither do we, because there is no Asteria server to get it from.

What it does

An encrypted vault

PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with 600,000 iterations wraps your master password; AES-GCM seals every item. The key stays in the keychain, behind Face ID or Touch ID.

Passkeys and one-time codes

Passkeys live in the same vault, and one-time codes are generated on device. AutoFill fills passwords and codes in other apps and on the web.

iCloud, not our cloud

Only ciphertext goes to your private CloudKit database. A change on the iPhone arrives on the Mac, and neither of them asks an Asteria server for permission.

Apple Intelligence, discreetly

Describe a login and the on-device model drafts the name, site and username. Smart search finds items from a description. The security coach sees counts of findings, never a password, username, site or note.

Siri with manners

“Compose a login in Asteria Keys.” “Find in Asteria Keys.” “Lock Asteria Keys.” Siri can prepare a login, open an item, search titles and sites, and check what needs review. It never speaks a password.

Security you can see

Reused, short and old passwords, logins without a one-time code, and a score that does not congratulate you for opening the app. Fix them one at a time.

The part where we are honest

No account. No Asteria server. No analytics. No ads. No third-party SDK. If you forget your master password and your recovery key, nobody can open the vault. That is not a support limitation; it is the product working.

Private Cloud Compute is compiled in, gated and off until Apple grants the managed entitlement. The on-device model runs everything by default. We are not going to pretend otherwise.

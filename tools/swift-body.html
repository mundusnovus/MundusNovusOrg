Swift: Zero to Hero

From the first line to the App Store. The compiler is the judge; the AI is the teaching assistant.

A complete Swift course for iPhone, iPad and Mac: 173 lessons in 10 chapters, 83 exercises, 82 quizzes, and a small on-device engine that checks your code without sending it anywhere. The Mac uses its own Swift compiler. The last chapter is the Modern Apple one, which is a polite way of saying Apple Intelligence, App Intents, SwiftData + CloudKit, Liquid Glass, evaluations and Live Activities.

What 2.0 does

Checked on your device

On iPhone and iPad, a parser, a teaching-subset interpreter and the lesson’s own tests check your work in the same process. No network, no account, no install id. The Mac uses the real Swift compiler when it is available.

An honest limit

61 of the 83 exercises are checked fully on device. The other 22 open as Xcode labs, with the tests and starting code in front of you. Nothing is silently marked wrong. Four of them use Codable or async runtime features outside the small engine; the rest are parser or language features that need the full compiler.

Apple Intelligence, without the answers

A coach explains a compiler error without rewriting your code. A review of a working solution suggests one small improvement. A recap turns a lesson into three questions. You can ask for a mini-lesson and keep it in My Lessons, with a scratch editor to try the code.

A plan, not a guilt trip

The weekly plan is chosen from your progress alone. Apple Intelligence only writes a sentence of encouragement, so it can never point at a lesson that does not exist. Fifteen, thirty, forty-five or sixty minutes a day. You can also just read the next lesson, like an adult.

Siri, Spotlight and Handoff

“Continue my Swift course in Swift Zero to Hero.” “How far am I in Swift Zero to Hero.” Open a lesson from Spotlight, carry the open lesson between devices, and find a milestone from Siri. The course stays in the app; the account does not exist.

Progress that follows you

Lessons, code, checklist ticks, streaks and badges sync through your private iCloud. Accent colour and code size follow you too. If iCloud is unavailable, everything still works on the device and Settings says so, in words rather than a spinner.

The free part

The course is also free on the web at Asteria Learn. Read, write real code, press Run, and find out whether the compiler agrees with you. It usually does not. That is the lesson.

The app has been submitted for App Store review. A store link will appear here when it is available. The web course is available now and does not need an account, which is increasingly rare enough to mention twice.

No ads. Ever.

A proprietary Asteria Technology app. No ads, no analytics. An Asteria Account is optional, and Asteria’s server is only used for Asteria Intelligence, after you say yes. The course stays free on the web. The app is the version that fits in your pocket and knows when to stop talking.
